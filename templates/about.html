About Jim.Tennis

The problem

If you've ever run a parks league team, you know the drill. Every week you're chasing players on WhatsApp, trying to work out who's available, juggling multiple teams, and hoping you haven't accidentally picked someone who's on holiday. It means hours of manual admin — messaging every player individually, keeping track of replies in your head or on scraps of paper, and crossing your fingers that you haven't double-booked anyone.

Jim.Tennis was built to make all of that go away.

What does it actually do?

Players manage their own availability. Each player gets a personal link — no login, no app to download, it just works in any browser on any phone. They see the next four weeks of fixtures and mark themselves as available or unavailable as they go. The idea is that players check in every week or so, keeping things current so captains aren't chasing stale information.

Captains see everything on one screen. For any given fixture, you get a colour-coded view of every player in your club: who's available, who might be if needed, and who's definitely not. No spreadsheets, no scrolling back through group chats.

Team selection is straightforward. Pick your teams from the available players, and the system keeps track of who's playing where. It warns you if you pick someone who has already played for another team that week. It is a warning only: you can still choose them.

It handles the league rules for you. In the second half of the season, the BHPLTA lock-in rule means that a player who has appeared five or more times for a higher division team can no longer play for a lower one. The system tracks all of this automatically — it shows you how many appearances each player has, shows when someone is close to being locked in, and switches off selecting a player for a lower team once they have reached five. No more end-of-season surprises.

Results and standings update automatically. Match results are imported from the BHPLTA website, so your club's standings stay up to date without anyone having to type scores in manually.

Minimal personal data. The only personal information stored about players is their names — no email addresses, no phone numbers, no passwords for players. As members of a sports club playing in a public league, some level of visibility is expected, and the system is designed to stay well within that.

Guides

New to the app? Your link is a one-page sheet for players: setting availability, turning on notifications and adding the app to a phone.

Captains and administrators have their own guides inside the app. Open the guides (sign in first). They always match the version your club is running.

Built by
James Hartt
Creator & Developer

Jim.Tennis was designed and built by James Hartt at St Ann's Tennis Club for the Brighton & Hove Parks League. The code is free and open source — each club runs its own copy of the software with its own data. Nobody outside your club has access to that copy or its data.

github.com/jameshartt

Tournament management by
CourtHive
Charles Allen, Primary Developer

Cup and tournament management is developed and maintained by CourtHive, with the primary development effort by Charles Allen. CourtHive provides tournament scheduling, draw management, and live scoring.

Where your club has switched tournaments on, they run on tournament software that is part of your club's own copy of Jim.Tennis. It is built on top of CourtHive.

Acknowledgements

The Brighton & Hove Parks League Tennis Association (BHPLTA) organises the parks league that Jim.Tennis is built around. Match card data is imported from the BHPLTA website.

Jim.Tennis was first built for, and used by, St Ann's Tennis Club.

Questions

Questions about this site go to your own club's administrators. The project itself lives on GitHub: github.com/jameshartt/jim-dot-tennis.

Why "Jim.Tennis"?

The name comes from the creator's on-court habit of shouting "JIM!" at himself — loudly, earnestly, and with varying degrees of composure — as a form of positive self-talk. Whether it actually helps is up for debate, but it never fails to entertain the nearby courts.

It comes from a genuine love for what is, in our humble opinion, the best sport in the world.
